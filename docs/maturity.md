# Maturity levels

Coppice is useful from the first four files. Each level adds something only once the one before is working. Moving up a level is a choice, not a goal: plenty of people should stay at level 1.

| Level | You add | You get | Setup time |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0 — Remembered** | `START-HERE.md`, `soul.md`, `setup.md`, one tasks file, one recent-sessions file | An assistant that knows who you are and what's live, without being re-briefed each session | 15 min |
| **1 — Organised** | `memory/projects.md`, numbered project folders with logs, the end-of-session routine, `new-project` and `end-of-session` skills | Work that survives between sessions, with its history in one place and a lean index on top | +30 min |
| **2 — Tended** | `archive-trim` and `doctor` scheduled tasks, the heartbeat, the learning log | A workspace that keeps itself small and tells you when something's drifting or has stopped running | +30 min |
| **3 — Proactive** | Morning briefing with escalating flags, weekly review, several contexts, and optionally a knowledge wiki and session capture | An assistant that starts the day with you, notices what's stuck, and improves its own way of working through approved changes | +1–2 hrs |

## Signs you're ready for the next level

- **0 → 1:** you've mentioned the same piece of work in three or more sessions, or you've lost track of a decision and why it was made.
- **1 → 2:** a file read at startup has noticeably grown, or you've forgotten the end-of-session routine more than once.
- **2 → 3:** you open most days by asking "what's on today?", or things keep getting stuck without anyone noticing.

## Signs you've gone too far

- The morning briefing is something you skim past.
- The learning log has more entries than you can remember the point of.
- You're maintaining the system more than it's helping.

If so, turn something off. The weekly review's question about the system ("which file did we read most this week that told us least?") is there to catch this.
